Beej Country, Language & Channel Filter for YouTube

Hide YouTube videos by the channel's declared country, by the language of the content, or by blocking a channel outright.

Blocked countries

Matched against the country a creator declares on their channel. It is optional, so many channels have none — those are shown unless you tick the box below.

Blocked languages

Detected on-device from the video title and channel name. Non-Latin scripts are identified outright; Latin-script titles go through Chrome's language detector, and anything it can't call confidently is left visible.

Where to apply it

Always block

Individual channels to hide regardless of country or language — for the ones that slip through the rules. Fastest way to add one is to right-click a channel name on YouTube and choose “Block this YouTube channel”, or right-click the page while watching a video. One per line — a handle (@example) or a channel id (UCHnyf…).

Always allow

Channels here are never hidden, whatever the rules say. A channel cannot be in both lists — adding it to one removes it from the other. One per line — a handle (@veritasium) or a channel id (UCHnyf…).

Optional: YouTube Data API key

Not required. Beej reads the country straight from YouTube's own About page. A key is only a fallback for channels where that fails — it costs 1 quota unit per lookup against a 10,000/day allowance.

What has been blocked

Videos Beej actually hid, newest first — by language, by country, and by the channels you blocked by hand. Recorded as it happens, capped at the most recent 400, and stored only on this machine. Nothing is recorded retroactively, so this starts from the moment a rule is added.

Channels Beej has seen

Every channel whose country has been looked up, and what your rules would do the next time one of its videos appears. This is a prediction, not a history — a channel can say “would hide” here and be absent from What has been blocked above, because it was looked up before you added the rule, or because no video of its has come past since.

Language is judged per video title rather than per channel, so it is not part of the prediction here.

Cache

Beej resolves each channel's declared country once and remembers it for 30 days, so it never looks the same channel up twice. This counts how many channels it has seen, not how many are blocked — the second number is how many of them declare a country at all, since the field is optional.
